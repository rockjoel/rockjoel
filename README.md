## Before — pre-AI craft

Years on the bench and outdoors: explore a physical problem, build a fast proof-of-concept, adapt what’s available, measure, document limits, then iterate. Buyer-facing label: **prototype bench test & guided low-voltage hardware**.

### How that work looked

- **Low-voltage integration (5 / 12 / 24 V)** — plug-ready supplies, enclosures, quick connectors; Wi‑Fi / beacon / sensor nodes. Not licensed mains/electrician work; no wall wiring or panel certification claims.
- **Prototype QA** — assemble, instrument, run pass / fail / partial reports so a remote partner can decide the next step.
- **Embedded & sensing** — Arduino / ESP-class boards, sensors, logging to CSV / simple backends; firmware and field bring-up by reading datasheets, forums, and breaking things on the bench.
- **Tree / outdoor sensing prototypes** — vibration + temperature/humidity style nodes intended for outdoor monitoring, with offline decode/logging tooling (architecture documented; no site maps or private installs published here).
- **Remote monitoring stacks** — multi-sensor setups (e.g. hydroponic / barge-style research platforms): MCU + SBC, environmental sensors, camera, cellular uplink concepts, solar-aware power — collaboration-era work, described at capability level only.
- **CAD → physical** — 3D modelling for compact mechanical parts, digital fabrication / CNC as a shop skill (not industrial certification), lithophane-style light-through prints and small production experiments.
- **Mobility-aid electronics prototype (private details)** — CAD + early PCB/MCU work on a compact electromechanical module; photos and product specifics stay offline.

Public artifact for that era: **[prototype-qa-notes](https://github.com/rockjoel/prototype-qa-notes)** — QA / bring-up templates and technical background (Arduino, ESP, CAD/CNC, sensors).

Other client work, family commerce, and raw evidence remain private.
